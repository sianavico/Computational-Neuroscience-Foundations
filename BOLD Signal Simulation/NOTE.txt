**Project 1: Simulating Neural Activity and BOLD Signals Using Python**

In this project, we simulated how neural activity in the brain produces the BOLD (Blood Oxygen Level Dependent) signal measured by fMRI. We started by creating artificial neural events at specific times and then generated a Hemodynamic Response Function (HRF) using the gamma distribution. The HRF represents the delayed blood-oxygenation response following neural activity. Using convolution, we combined the neural events with the HRF to produce a simulated BOLD signal. We also experimented with the HRF parameters `a` and `scale` to understand how changes in response timing and duration affect the resulting signal, particularly when multiple neural events occur close together and their BOLD responses overlap.

Next, we introduced random Gaussian noise into the simulated BOLD signal to represent measurement fluctuations commonly found in real fMRI data, such as scanner noise and physiological variations. We compared clean and noisy BOLD signals at different noise levels to understand how noise can make neural responses harder to recognize. Throughout the project, we used Python libraries including NumPy, SciPy, and Matplotlib for numerical calculations, HRF generation, convolution, and visualization. This project helped us understand the relationship between neural activity and the indirect signals recorded by fMRI, providing a foundation for our next project: estimating neural events from noisy BOLD measurements.
